Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Leeks Terminal — signals, data, methodology, and how to use the platform effectively.

What does BUY / SELL / WAIT mean? SIGNALS

+

BUY: The model detects a mean reversion entry point — price is near a support zone, momentum is turning bullish, and the risk/reward ratio meets our threshold. A long position is signalled with a specific trigger price, target, and stop-loss.

SELL: The model detects a shorting opportunity — price is near a resistance zone, momentum is turning bearish, and the R:R ratio is favourable. A short position is signalled with trigger, target, and stop.

WAIT: No high-confidence signal. Price is in a neutral zone, or market regime conditions (e.g., low volume, high volatility) are not met. This is not a negative view — it simply means the model doesn't have an edge right now.

What is the Trigger Price? SIGNALS

+

The trigger price is the entry condition — the level at which the signal becomes actionable. This is typically slightly above support (for BUY) or slightly below resistance (for SELL) to confirm the zone has been respected. You do not enter immediately; you wait for the price to reach or confirm at the trigger level.

What is the R:R ratio? SIGNALS

+

R:R stands for Risk:Reward ratio. A 2.0 R:R means you are targeting 2× your stop-loss distance as profit. For example, if your stop-loss is $1 away from entry, a 2.0 R:R means your target is $2 away. We only show signals with R:R ≥ 1.5 on the platform.

R:R = (Target − Entry) / (Entry − Stop). The platform shows this as a single number (e.g., 2.14).

Why are signals on Leeks Terminal different from broker alerts? SIGNALS

+

Broker alerts are usually price-level triggers — they fire when a stock crosses a price. Leeks Terminal signals are contextual: they consider support/resistance zones, market phase, volume profile, and R:R ratio. A signal means the model has identified a specific entry with defined risk management, not just "price is above X."

Why is the data from yesterday, not today? DATA

+

Leeks Terminal uses end-of-day (EOD) closing data. The signals are generated based on the previous day's close, which is the standard for swing trading and positional strategies. Intraday data is not displayed because the strategies are not designed for intraday trading.

If you are checking the site in the morning, the data is from the previous close — this is intentional and correct.

What are Support and Resistance levels? DATA

+

Support (S1–S4): Price zones where buying has historically absorbed selling pressure. The model identifies these from recent swing lows and round-number clusters.

Resistance (R1–R4): Price zones where selling has historically capped rallies. Identified from recent swing highs and volume nodes.

These are not fixed lines — they are zones. A breach of support doesn't always mean the stock will fall further; it means the balance of supply and demand has shifted.

What is the Trading Methodology? METHOD

+

Leeks Terminal uses a mean reversion + momentum confirmation approach across three market regimes:

  • Downtrend Active: Focus on SHORT signals; mean reversion to resistance zones
  • Uptrend: Focus on BUY signals; momentum confirms support holds
  • Base Building: Mixed signals; wait for range break

Full methodology details are available on the Methodology page.

How were the backtest results generated? METHOD

+

The Backtest page shows simulated historical performance using end-of-day data. Each strategy was backtested over a defined lookback period with these assumptions:

  • Entry at signal close price (not intraday)
  • Exit at T+1, T+2, or T+3 close (configurable)
  • Broker fee: 0.25% HK / 0.05% US (approximate)
  • No slippage modelled (conservative)

These are simulations, not live results. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
